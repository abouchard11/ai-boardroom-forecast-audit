# Claims and limitations

## Synthetic roles, not endorsements

Alex Bouchard created every executive, operator, legal, marketing and investor
seat as an AI role simulation used to challenge a forecast. No real person
participated in the synthetic boardroom. The outputs must not be described as
validation by any actual event-company employee, operator, attorney, marketing
firm or investment firm.

## Forecasts, not operating results

The underlying Houston festival concept had real commercial intent and was
planned around the 2026 FIFA World Cup. The revenue and profit figures modeled
here are forecasts from that planning process, not operating results.

Most expense lines were grounded in concrete project-planning inputs. “Real”
here means a bona fide estimate, quote, rate, staffing calculation or operating
assumption used to plan the proposed event; it does not necessarily mean the
amount was ultimately incurred, paid or fixed by an executed contract. A
smaller subset of inflated, duplicated or incorrectly scaled expenses was
normalized during review.

## The simulation is a reconstruction

The historical repository documents scenario and sensitivity analysis but no
committed Monte Carlo or regression implementation. The analysis in this public
repository was rebuilt in July 2026 from preserved scenario points and disclosed
uncertainty assumptions.

## Regression is explanatory, not causal

Standardized coefficients rank which simulated inputs are associated with
profit variation inside this model. They do not establish causal effects in the
real festival market.

## Reconstructed probability distributions are illustrative

The historical expense anchors are grounded in the project’s planning model.
The probability distributions layered over those anchors are judgment calls,
not statistically estimated from a large sample of comparable Houston
festivals. They exist to make execution uncertainty and definition risk visible.

## No investment recommendation

This repository is a product-engineering and decision-systems case study. It is
not an offer, forecast assurance, accounting opinion or investment advice.

## OpenClaw attribution and privacy

OpenClaw's upstream runtime was created by Peter Steinberger and its open-source
community. Alex Bouchard used and modified a private working copy and created a
separate personal operating workspace. This case does not claim authorship of
the upstream project.

The raw ClawdBot, OpenClaw and Diggs repositories are not public artifacts for
this case. They contain combinations of upstream code, personal agent context,
historical credentials, operational identifiers and internal business data.
Only sanitized architecture and attribution-safe evidence are reused here.

## Solo developer does not mean model-free

“Solo developer” means Alex Bouchard was the only human product
designer/developer and final decision-maker. AI models and coding agents
generated substantial research, code, review and documentation under his
direction. Where commit metadata identifies a model as co-author, that
co-authorship should remain visible.
