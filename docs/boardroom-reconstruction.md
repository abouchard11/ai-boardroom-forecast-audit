# Boardroom reconstruction

This document reconstructs the decision process preserved in the private
Synesthetic Soundscapes planning repository.

Alex Bouchard created every boardroom seat as an AI role. No actual executive,
operator, attorney, marketer or investor participated in the synthetic panel.

The underlying project was intended as a real Houston festival venture timed
with the 2026 FIFA World Cup. The panel evaluated whether that proposed venture
deserved capital and execution; it was not reviewing results from an event that
had already occurred.

## What the historical artifacts support

The final workbook names six review functions:

| Seat | Decision lens | Examples of pressure applied |
|---|---|---|
| CFO | Unit economics and pricing | Reduced the FIFA module, challenged pricing and required a larger contingency reserve |
| COO | Operations and safety | Increased security and medical staffing; identified major underfunding |
| Industry analyst | Market realism | Challenged Year 1 sell-through and comparable-event assumptions |
| Legal counsel | Compliance and liability | Added permitting, insurance and entertainment-law costs |
| Marketing strategist | Acquisition realism | Treated Year 1 as an awareness-buying year rather than free organic demand |
| Investor diligence | Downside and capital | Tested return structure, cash-flow timing and capital protection |

The operating-partner deck also refers to CEO, CFO, COO and investor personas.
The role roster therefore evolved across artifacts. This public reconstruction
uses the six functional lenses in the final workbook rather than pretending the
historical room had a perfectly stable cast.

## What changed after the review

- The initial approximately $65.9M profit projection was rejected.
- The public-facing target became a $5M–$7M base-case range.
- Grounded expense inputs were retained while inflated, duplicated or
  incorrectly scaled expense lines were normalized.
- Security and medical costs increased materially.
- Sponsorship was reduced to reflect a first-year event with no track record.
- Streaming pay-per-view revenue was excluded.
- The FIFA-related module was reduced to a conservative secondary contribution.
- Contingency increased to an 18% base assumption.

Most of the expense structure represented real planning estimates rather than
numbers invented to force a desired profit. The largest early distortion was on
the revenue side, including an approximately $51.2M on-site F&B projection.

## What the review did not solve

The corrected workbook still used two expense definitions:

- $18.872M was subtracted to produce the reported $6.422M base profit.
- $22.269M was displayed elsewhere as total expense after an 18% contingency.

Subtracting the displayed total expense from $25.294M of revenue produces
$3.025M, not $6.422M. The difference is $3.397M—the contingency reserve.

The case-study lesson is therefore not “six agents reached truth.” It is:

> Adversarial roles can improve assumptions while a weak source of truth still
> allows incompatible definitions to survive.

## Historical versus reconstructed analysis

The private repository contains scenario tables, sensitivity tables and a
tornado-chart implementation. It does not contain code or logged outputs for a
Monte Carlo simulation or regression.

The simulation and standardized regression in this public repository were
reconstructed in July 2026 to make the remembered decision method reproducible.
They are not presented as untouched December 2025 artifacts.
